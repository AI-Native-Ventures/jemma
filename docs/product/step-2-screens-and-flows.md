## Getting in

Sign up with a phone number and a one-time code, then pick a role: Parent, Teacher, or Learner (Grade 8 to 12). Younger children never sign up: a parent adds them.

1. **Teacher:** school, subjects and grades, then create a class. Pick a map from the shared library or upload their own curriculum, approve it, add learners (type names or snap the register), and share the class code.
2. **Parent:** add a child (name, grade, school optional). "Got a class code from a teacher?" Enter it or skip. No teacher: pick subject maps from the shared library. First step: snap a test, or run the 10 minute "find my level" check.
3. **One code per teacher:** a primary child has one class teacher. A high school child may have 8 subject teachers, so each teacher's code adds that subject to the same child.
4. **Teen (Grade 8 to 12):** the parent sends an invite from the child's settings. The teen signs in on their own phone and lands in the same child space. Invite first because children's data needs a parent's consent (South Africa's POPIA treats under-18s as children; not verified for other countries, needs a legal check).
5. **Shared family phone:** children added by a parent appear on a "Who's learning?" picker, each child behind their own PIN.

## Key flows

**Snap and confirm (parent, also teen)**

1. Tap Snap. Pick what it is: test, homework, textbook page, or report card.
2. Pick the subject, then take one photo per page.
3. Jemma reads it and shows a confirm screen: each question with what Jemma read and how it marked it.
4. Tap anything wrong to fix it, then Confirm.
5. Saved: "2 gaps found. Tonight's lesson is ready."

**Bulk snap (teacher)**

1. Tap Snap. Pick a test or create one (name, subject, topic).
2. Answer key: snap the memo, or approve the key Jemma drafts.
3. Snap papers back to back with a counter ("23 of 38"). Jemma reads in the background.
4. Needs you: only the papers Jemma is unsure about (unclear name, messy handwriting).
5. Results: class gaps update and linked parents get "Tonight's lesson is ready."

**A learning session (child)**

1. Warm-up: 2 quick questions on gaps fixed earlier.
2. Learn: one gap in one format (4 cards, 1 diagram, or a 3 minute audio memo).
3. Try: 5 questions, no right or wrong shown. Jemma quietly eases the next question after a miss.
4. Results: "Let's look at these 2 together", with why and the right way.
5. Progress: the gap moves stage, effort is praised, and the parent gets a summary.

## Patterns every screen shares

- **Needs you:** one list of everything Jemma needs a person for (confirm a scan, approve a key or map, check unsure papers). Top of the parent and teacher homes. Empty means nothing to do.
- **Evidence on tap:** any claim ("struggles with fractions") opens the test, the question and the photo it came from.
- **Snap in the middle:** the centre of the tab bar for teachers and parents; a button on Today for Grade 8 and up.
- **Space in the header:** the class or child name sits in the header. Tap it to switch. Gear for that space's settings.
- **Headline first:** each screen leads with one sentence from Jemma, details on tap. One job per screen.

## Laptop and phone

Everyone gets both, and everything works on both. The phone is for snapping and checking progress; the laptop is easier for real work, especially for Grade 8 to 12.

| Job | Phone | Laptop |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Snap tests and pages | Best: the camera | Upload scans or PDFs, or scan a QR code to snap with the phone and watch the pages land on the laptop |
| Check progress | Best | Works |
| Notifications | Best | Works |
| Learning sessions | Short sessions, Grade 1 to 7 | Best for Grade 8 to 12: typing, working out, bigger diagrams |
| Ask Jemma | Quick questions | Best: chat beside the textbook page or lesson |
| Teacher: review papers | Works | Best: paper photo and Jemma's reading side by side |
| Teacher: curriculum maps and class reports | Works | Best |

Laptop layout (my call): a left sidebar replaces the bottom tabs, lists and details sit side by side (Learners list beside the selected child), and the space switcher stays at the top. Same tabs, same names, same order, so nothing is relearned.
