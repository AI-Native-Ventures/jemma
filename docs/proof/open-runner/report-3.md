## Gap 1 (most common)

**Topic:** Adding fractions with different denominators
**Mistake:** Adds the tops and the bottoms
**Count:** 8 learner answers (from code-computed gaps)
**Example from code:** 1/2 + 1/4 = 2/6

### Affected learners
- Amina - Q1 wrote 2/6, Q2 wrote 3/9, Q4 wrote 3 2/6
- Naledi - Q1 wrote 2/6, Q4 wrote 3 2/6
- Thabo - Q1 wrote 2/6, Q2 wrote 3/9, Q4 wrote 3 2/6

### Why
Learners add the tops and add the bottoms because they treat each fraction as two separate whole numbers joined by a line, so they combine each part on its own.

### Missing basic
Equivalent fractions

### Explanation (teacher says aloud)
To add fractions with different denominators, first make the denominators the same using equivalent fractions. Then add only the tops, and keep the bottom the same.

## Reteach - Gap 1

**Title:** Adding fractions with different denominators
**Audience:** Amina, Thabo and Naledi
**You need:** Fraction strips, a fraction wall chart, and squared paper
**Total minutes:** 10

### Steps
1. (3 min) Show on the fraction wall that 1/2 equals 2/4, so 1/2 + 1/4 becomes 2/4 + 1/4 = 3/4.
2. (3 min) With fraction strips, build 2/3 and 1/6, then turn 2/3 into 4/6 so both strips have sixths, and add to get 5/6.
3. (2 min) Practise 1/2 + 1/4 together on squared paper, shading each fraction and counting the total quarters.
4. (2 min) Learners try 2/3 + 1/6 on their own while you check they made same denominators first.

### Exit question
What is 1/2 + 1/4, and why is the answer not 2/6?
